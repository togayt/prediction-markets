Kalshi and Polymarket are the two most talked-about prediction markets right now. They approach the space in completely different ways, and the right one for you depends almost entirely on where you live and whether you hold crypto.

*Educational content only, not financial advice.*

## The short answer

- **In the US:** Kalshi. Polymarket is not available to US persons.
- **Outside the US, comfortable with crypto:** Polymarket.
- **Outside the US, want fiat simplicity:** Kalshi if it is available in your country, otherwise Crypto.com or check regional options.

## Regulation

**Kalshi** is regulated by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), the same body that oversees US futures markets. That means customer funds are held under regulated rules, contracts are legally defined financial instruments, and there is a formal complaint process if something goes wrong.

**Polymarket** operates via smart contracts on the Polygon blockchain. There is no central regulator overseeing it in the traditional sense. The CFTC has previously taken enforcement action against Polymarket regarding US access, which is why US persons are blocked today.

If legal certainty and regulatory protection matter to you, Kalshi wins this category outright.

## Market selection

**Polymarket** has the larger catalogue. You will find markets on:
- Every major global election
- Central bank decisions in the US, EU, UK, and beyond
- Crypto prices, ETF decisions, protocol launches
- AI model releases and tech milestones
- Sports championships, award shows, cultural moments

**Kalshi** covers a strong range of US-focused topics:
- US elections, congressional votes, and policy
- Federal Reserve rate decisions
- Economic data releases (inflation, jobs, GDP)
- Weather, some sports, and tech events

If breadth of markets is your priority and you are outside the US, Polymarket has the edge.

## Liquidity

Both platforms have liquid markets on major events, but Polymarket consistently sees higher dollar volume on the biggest questions. Presidential elections and major Fed decisions on Polymarket regularly attract tens of millions of dollars. Kalshi has grown significantly but tends to have thinner books on niche topics.

Thin liquidity means wider spreads and more price impact when you trade. On popular markets, both platforms are fine. On smaller markets, check the order book before you commit.

## How you fund your account

**Kalshi:** USD via bank transfer (ACH) or debit card. Standard financial account setup, similar to a brokerage. No crypto required.

**Polymarket:** USDC only. You need a crypto wallet (they support several including MetaMask and embedded wallets). You will need to acquire USDC first, which typically means buying it on a crypto exchange and transferring it in.

This is the biggest practical difference for most people. Kalshi is as easy to fund as opening a savings account. Polymarket requires crypto literacy.

## Fees

**Kalshi:** charges a per-contract trading fee, calculated as a percentage of potential payout. Exact rates vary by market. No deposit or withdrawal fees for ACH. Check their current fee page before trading.

**Polymarket:** historically very low or zero trading fees on many markets. You pay small Polygon gas fees (typically under $0.01 per transaction). The main cost is the spread on less liquid markets.

For active traders on large markets, Polymarket's fee structure tends to be cheaper. For occasional traders, the difference is minimal.

## Interface and ease of use

**Kalshi** has a cleaner, more traditional interface. If you have used a stock broker or investment app, you will feel at home.

**Polymarket** has improved significantly but still requires you to manage a crypto wallet, which adds steps for beginners.

## Bottom line

If you are in the US, the choice is made for you: Kalshi. If you are outside the US and crypto-comfortable, Polymarket's depth is hard to beat. Neither platform is universally better: they serve genuinely different audiences.
