Kalshi is currently the only federally regulated prediction market exchange in the United States, operating under oversight from the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC). That regulatory status makes it the default choice for US-based traders who want a compliant, mainstream-friendly platform.

*This article is for education only and is not financial advice.*

## What is Kalshi?

Launched in 2021, Kalshi offers event contracts: yes/no binary contracts tied to real-world outcomes across politics, economics, weather, and more. Because it is CFTC-regulated, it can legally serve most US states and accept USD deposits through standard bank transfers and cards, without requiring crypto.

## What can you trade?

Kalshi lists markets across categories including:

- **Economics**: inflation data, Federal Reserve rate decisions, unemployment figures
- **Politics**: elections, congressional votes, and policy milestones
- **Finance**: stock index levels, crypto prices, and earnings outcomes
- **Weather and climate**: temperature records, storm activity
- **Tech and culture**: product launch dates, subscriber milestones

The catalogue changes regularly. Active markets have better liquidity; niche markets can have wide spreads and thin order books.

## How does trading work?

Each market is a binary yes/no question. Contracts settle at $1 if the outcome occurs and $0 if it does not. You buy yes contracts if you think an outcome is likely, or no contracts if you think it is not. The price in cents implies the market's probability estimate: a 60¢ yes contract implies roughly a 60% probability.

Kalshi uses a central limit order book, so your order fills when a counterparty takes the other side. You can place limit orders at the price you want or market orders that fill immediately at the best available price.

## Fees

Kalshi charges a trading fee on each transaction, calculated as a percentage of the potential payout. The exact rate depends on the market; check the platform's current fee schedule before trading. There are no deposit or withdrawal fees for standard bank transfers, though processing times apply.

## Who is Kalshi best for?

- **US residents** who want a regulated, compliant platform
- **Traders** comfortable with traditional financial account setups (KYC verification, bank transfers)
- **People new to prediction markets** who want a familiar, well-supported interface
- **Those focused on US politics and economic data** where Kalshi's catalogue is strongest

## Who might prefer something else?

- **Non-US users**: Kalshi's availability outside the US is limited; check eligibility before signing up
- **Crypto-native traders** who prefer blockchain-based settlement
- **Those wanting very large market selection**: Polymarket often has more markets, especially for global events

## Bottom line

Kalshi's CFTC regulation is a genuine differentiator for US traders who want legal certainty. The platform is polished, uses familiar payment methods, and covers core topics well. Liquidity can be thin on less-popular markets, and the fee structure requires attention on smaller trades. For most US-based beginners, it is the lowest-friction starting point.

*Availability and rules vary by state and age. Always verify your eligibility on Kalshi's official site before creating an account.*
