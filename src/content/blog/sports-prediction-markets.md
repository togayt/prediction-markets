Sports are one of the most popular categories on prediction markets. If you have ever used a sportsbook, you will find sports prediction markets familiar: but there are meaningful differences worth understanding before you trade.

*Educational content only, not financial advice.*

## How sports prediction markets differ from sportsbooks

Traditional sportsbooks offer odds expressed as lines (e.g. -110, +200, 1.91). You bet against the house, which takes a cut in the form of the vig (the difference between fair odds and what they offer).

Prediction markets work differently. You trade contracts with other participants, not against the house. The platform takes a small fee on trades, but prices are set by supply and demand among traders. In theory, this means prices are more information-efficient: they reflect what many traders collectively believe, not what a bookmaker wants to offer.

In practice, for major events, the prices end up similar. For niche events, they can diverge significantly.

## What sports markets are available

Availability varies by platform and region. Common categories include:

**Major championships.** Super Bowl, NBA Finals, Stanley Cup, Champions League, World Cup, Premier League title. These attract the most volume and have the tightest spreads.

**Season-long markets.** Which team wins a division, who gets relegated, who finishes top. These stay open for months.

**Individual performance.** Award winners (MVP, Golden Boot, Ballon d'Or), statistical milestones, draft picks.

**Game-level outcomes.** Some platforms offer match-by-match markets, though these are less common than on traditional sportsbooks.

**Kalshi** has sports markets for US audiences where permitted under its regulatory framework. **Polymarket** covers global sports for non-US users and often has the deepest liquidity on major international events.

## The regulatory picture

Sports prediction markets sit in a grey area in some jurisdictions. Kalshi is CFTC-regulated but navigates which sports markets it can legally offer in the US. Polymarket is not available to US persons. Rules are evolving.

Always check what is legal in your location and what the platform's terms say before placing a sports trade.

## How to approach sports markets

**Know your sport deeply.** The traders setting prices on sports markets include hardcore fans and analysts. Casual knowledge of a sport is unlikely to give you an edge against that pool of informed participants.

**Look for market inefficiencies in lower-profile events.** Major league championship markets are priced by many sophisticated traders. A second-division football league or a niche sport may have fewer informed traders, which means more opportunity but also more risk from thin liquidity.

**Separate your fandom from your trading.** Supporting a team emotionally is normal. Assuming your team is likely to win because you want them to is one of the most expensive biases in sports prediction markets. Bet your honest probability assessment, not your heart.

**Account for how long until resolution.** A contract on "Team X wins the league" might not resolve for six months. Your money is tied up for that entire period. Make sure the potential return justifies the wait.

## Comparing to sports betting

If you are familiar with traditional sports betting, here are the key differences:

| | Prediction market | Traditional sportsbook |
|---|---|---|
| You bet against | Other traders | The house |
| Prices set by | Supply and demand | Bookmaker |
| Typical fee | Trading fee + spread | Vig (margin) built into odds |
| Regulation (US) | CFTC (Kalshi) | State-by-state gaming laws |
| Withdrawal | Standard financial | Gaming-specific rules |

Neither structure is inherently better. Prediction markets tend to be more price-efficient on heavily-traded events; traditional sportsbooks often have more market depth on individual games.

## The World Cup 2026

The FIFA World Cup is being held in the US, Canada, and Mexico in 2026. It will likely be one of the highest-volume sports prediction market events of the year. Markets will cover:

- Which country wins the tournament
- Group stage qualification
- Top scorer
- Individual match outcomes

Watch Kalshi (for US users) and Polymarket (for non-US users) as the tournament approaches for available markets. Check resolution criteria carefully: particularly for markets on group stages where tiebreaker rules can affect outcomes in unexpected ways.

*Sports prediction markets involve real financial risk. Only trade with money you can afford to lose and always verify legality in your jurisdiction.*
